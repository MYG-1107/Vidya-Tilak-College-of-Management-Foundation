Japanese writing guide

Hiragana, Katakana and Kanji: Japanese Writing Systems for Beginners

Japanese learners encounter multiple writing systems. Understanding what each script does can make beginner study much easier and reduce confusion.

Updated September 30, 2026 · Educational guide by Vidya Tilak College of Management Foundation

Hiragana

Hiragana is a core phonetic script used widely for native Japanese words, grammatical endings and beginner reading. It is usually the first script beginners learn.

Katakana

Katakana is another phonetic script. It is frequently used for loanwords, foreign names and certain onomatopoeic or emphasis contexts. Learning it early makes everyday reading more accessible.

Kanji

Kanji are characters used in Japanese writing. Beginners typically learn a controlled set appropriate to their level rather than trying to learn everything at once.

A sensible beginner order

  1. Learn hiragana systematically.
  2. Learn katakana and practise reading both scripts.
  3. Build everyday vocabulary in kana.
  4. Add level-appropriate kanji gradually.
  5. Read short sentences every day.

Why reading practice matters

Recognising scripts is only the beginning. Combine reading with vocabulary, grammar and listening so that the written forms connect to actual language use.
