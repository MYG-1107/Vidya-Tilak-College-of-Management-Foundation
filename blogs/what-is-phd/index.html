PhD • Doctoral Research

What is a PhD?

A PhD (Doctor of Philosophy) is a research degree in which a scholar conducts original research under the rules of a university or research institution. The final award depends on the institution’s regulations, evaluation and successful completion of the doctoral requirements.

How is a PhD different from a Master’s?

A Master’s is primarily a postgraduate taught or mixed-mode qualification. A PhD is centred on original research, a defined research problem, scholarly supervision and a doctoral thesis/dissertation evaluated under institutional rules.

What about PhD fees?

Doctoral fees vary widely. Some programmes are funded through fellowships or institutional support; others charge tuition and research-related costs. For example, Harvard’s 2026–27 GSAS page states that PhD students may receive grants covering tuition and fees as described in their individual financial support notices. Always check the funding terms of the specific programme.

After a PhD: what next?

Researchers can explore post-doctoral fellowships, research projects, faculty roles, industry research and other scholarly careers. Post-doctoral opportunities are host- and discipline-specific.

Research application checklist
  • Academic CV
  • Statement of research interest
  • Research proposal where required
  • Transcripts and certificates
  • References / recommendation letters
  • Entrance test / interview preparation
Research fit matters

University reputation alone does not determine suitability. Check supervisor fit, research infrastructure, funding, publication expectations and institutional rules.
