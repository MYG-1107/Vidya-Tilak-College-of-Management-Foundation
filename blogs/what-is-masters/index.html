What is a Master’s Degree?

A Master’s degree is a postgraduate qualification normally pursued after a Bachelor’s degree. It allows deeper study in a chosen subject and can support career progression, professional specialisation or future doctoral research.

What can a Master’s degree do?

Postgraduate study can deepen subject knowledge, develop advanced professional skills and create a pathway to research. Requirements differ by discipline and institution.

Can a Master’s lead to a PhD?

Yes, many doctoral programmes use a Master’s or another qualifying postgraduate degree as an entry point, but institutions set their own rules. Research preparation, entrance tests, proposals and interviews can form part of the process.
