Ethical hacking and cybersecurity are related, but they are not interchangeable. Cybersecurity is the wider practice of protecting systems, information and services. Ethical hacking is a controlled form of security testing in which an authorised person examines agreed systems for weaknesses. Permission, scope and reporting define whether testing is legitimate.

## Cybersecurity covers more than testing

Cybersecurity includes asset management, access controls, secure configuration, monitoring, incident response, recovery, governance, awareness and risk management. Organisations need people who can reduce the chance of incidents, detect suspicious activity, coordinate response and improve systems after problems are found.

This work can involve networks, cloud infrastructure, applications, identity, endpoint devices, policies and business processes.

## Ethical hacking requires clear permission

A penetration test or security assessment should have a written scope identifying systems, time window, allowed methods, exclusions, contact points and reporting expectations. The tester should not access unrelated accounts, extract unnecessary data or disrupt services. Findings should be documented with evidence, impact and practical remediation.

Practising on a public website or a system you do not own is not ethical hacking just because the intention is educational. Use a legal lab or an environment where you have explicit permission.

## Skills that overlap

Both areas benefit from networking, operating-system knowledge, scripting, web application fundamentals, identity and access management, security principles and clear technical writing. Ethical testers need to understand vulnerabilities and validation methods; defenders need to understand how weaknesses may be exploited so they can reduce risk.

## Which learning path may fit you?

- Choose a broad security foundation if interested in risk, monitoring, policy, response or secure operations.
- Explore authorised testing after building networking, operating-system and application foundations.
- Practise in controlled labs and document permission and scope.
- Learn to explain findings to technical and non-technical audiences.
- Compare defensive content, ethics, legal coverage and practical labs.

## Evaluate programmes carefully

A course should explain prerequisites, lab environment, assessment, instructor background and what is included in the fees. Be cautious of promises that a short course automatically makes a learner job-ready. Explore the [digital skills area](/digital-skills.html).
