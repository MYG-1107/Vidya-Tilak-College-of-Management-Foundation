Supply chains connect suppliers, manufacturers, warehouses, transport providers, retailers and customers. An **MBA in Supply Chain and Logistics Management** explores how organisations plan and coordinate the movement of goods, information and resources.

The specialisation may suit graduates interested in operations, procurement, distribution, inventory, planning or business process improvement. Programme titles and depth vary, so check the current university syllabus.

## What does supply chain management involve?

A supply chain begins before a product reaches a warehouse and continues through delivery and, in some industries, returns and recovery. Management decisions can involve supplier selection, demand planning, inventory, warehouse operations, transport, service levels, cost, risk and sustainability.

A logistics function often concentrates on storage, movement and delivery. Supply chain management has a wider end-to-end perspective, although organisations use the terms differently.

## Subjects to look for in an MBA

A curriculum may include:

- Operations and process management.
- Procurement and supplier relationship management.
- Inventory and warehouse fundamentals.
- Transportation and distribution planning.
- Demand forecasting and supply planning.
- Supply chain analytics and performance measurement.
- Project management, risk and sustainability.

Ask whether the course uses practical cases and teaches learners to interpret service, cost, quality and lead-time measures.

## Skills that make learning practical

Build spreadsheet skills, basic statistics, communication, process mapping and problem-solving. Familiarity with enterprise systems or analytics tools may be useful, but the course should explain concepts before expecting learners to use software.

A portfolio project could map a simple retail replenishment process, compare transport options using transparent assumptions, or analyse a public dataset. Document the source, method and limitations.

## Career areas to research

Possible areas include procurement support, inventory planning, logistics coordination, operations analysis, warehouse administration, supply chain project coordination and distribution planning. Roles vary across manufacturing, retail, healthcare, e-commerce and consumer goods. Employers may require experience with particular systems or industry processes.

## MBA Supply Chain vs a general MBA

A general MBA may provide a broad management foundation with limited operations electives. A specialised programme should provide more focused supply-chain learning. Compare the actual credits, projects and faculty rather than relying on the degree title.

## Programme comparison checklist

1. Confirm the awarding university and current programme title.
2. Compare the full syllabus and elective structure.
3. Look for practical projects in inventory, procurement or distribution.
4. Check quantitative prerequisites and the level of analytics.
5. Understand study mode, attendance and assessment.
6. Calculate the full fee, including examinations and other charges.
7. Verify recognition for the exact mode and academic session.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is logistics the same as supply chain management?

They are related but not always identical. Logistics often focuses on storage and movement, while supply chain management coordinates a broader network of activities from suppliers to customers.

### Do I need an engineering background?

Not necessarily for every MBA. Eligibility depends on the university. Some roles may value technical or industry experience, so review the entry rules and intended learning outcomes.

### Does the MBA guarantee a supply-chain job?

No. Employment depends on the employer's criteria, experience, skills and selection process.
