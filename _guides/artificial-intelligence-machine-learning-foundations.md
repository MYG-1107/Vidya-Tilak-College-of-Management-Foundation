Artificial intelligence is a broad field concerned with systems that perform tasks associated with perception, language, reasoning or decision support. Machine learning is one approach within AI: models learn patterns from data and use those patterns to produce outputs. Students should approach the subject as a combination of mathematics, programming, data understanding, evaluation and responsible use.

## Build the foundations before the tools

Programming helps learners express ideas and work with data. Mathematics supports linear algebra, calculus, probability and statistics. These subjects become important when students need to understand how models behave, why they make errors and when a result is unreliable.

Learn data structures, basic algorithms, exploratory data analysis and how to work with structured datasets. Practise documenting data sources and checking whether the data represents the intended problem.

## Understand the machine-learning workflow

A typical workflow starts with a clear question, gathers and prepares data, selects a method, trains a model and evaluates it on data that was not used for training. Students should understand overfitting, data leakage, baseline comparisons and appropriate metrics. A high score on one dataset does not automatically mean a model is useful in another setting.

Generative AI introduces concerns such as prompt design, source checking, privacy, hallucinations, intellectual property and human review. Responsible use means knowing when a person must verify or override an output.

## Create small, explainable projects

Begin with a classification or prediction exercise using a public, non-sensitive dataset. Compare a simple baseline with a more complex model. Explain what the model can and cannot infer, where the data came from and what could cause unfair or inaccurate results. Avoid using personal or confidential data without clear permission.

## Questions to ask when choosing a course

- Does the syllabus include programming, statistics and data preparation?
- Are model evaluation and limitations taught?
- Do learners complete projects and explain their choices?
- Is responsible AI, privacy and human oversight covered?
- Are prerequisites, fees, duration and assessment clearly stated?
