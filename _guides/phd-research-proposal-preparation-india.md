A clear **PhD research proposal** explains what you want to investigate, why the topic matters, what is already known and how you plan to study the problem. It is not simply a long essay about an area of interest. It is a reasoned plan that a university or research committee can evaluate.

Universities may prescribe different formats, word limits and submission stages. Always follow the current instructions issued by the institution to which you are applying.

## 1. Start with a researchable problem

Begin with an area that interests you, then narrow it to a question that can be investigated with available time, evidence and methods. A topic such as “technology in education” is too broad by itself. A more focused question identifies a population or context, a particular issue and a possible relationship or process to study.

A strong question should be understandable, appropriately scoped and capable of being investigated ethically.

## 2. Explain the context and rationale

Describe the practical or scholarly issue that motivates the study. Explain who may be affected and why the question is worth investigating. Avoid claiming that a study will solve a large social or organisational problem before you have evidence.

Your rationale should connect the problem to the existing research.

## 3. Review relevant literature

A literature review maps the main ideas, methods and findings already published on the topic. It should compare sources, not merely list summaries. Identify where authors agree, where findings differ, which populations or settings have been studied and what limitations remain.

Keep a reference manager or structured source log. Record the author, year, title, publication, DOI or stable URL, research method and notes. Cite original sources accurately and never invent references.

## 4. State objectives and research questions

Use a small number of connected objectives. Each objective should support the central research question. Make sure the proposed data and methods can realistically address them.

Where relevant, formulate hypotheses only when the research design supports testing them.

## 5. Choose a suitable methodology

Explain whether the study is qualitative, quantitative, mixed-methods, conceptual or another recognised design. Describe the proposed data sources, sampling approach, instruments, analysis method and limitations.

Methodology must fit the question. A survey is not automatically the best method, and a large sample does not correct a poorly designed instrument.

## 6. Address ethics and feasibility

Consider informed consent, privacy, data security, vulnerable participants, conflicts of interest and the permissions you may need. Estimate access to participants or datasets, equipment, software, funding and supervisory expertise.

## 7. Prepare a realistic timeline

A proposal may include milestones for literature review, research design, ethics review, data collection, analysis, writing and revision. Treat the timeline as a plan that may be revised with the supervisor and university.

## Suggested proposal structure

| Section | What to include |
|---|---|
| Working title | Specific and understandable topic |
| Background | Context and research problem |
| Rationale | Why the question matters |
| Literature review | What is known and what remains unclear |
| Aim and objectives | What the study will investigate |
| Research questions | Questions the method can address |
| Methodology | Design, data, sampling and analysis |
| Ethics | Consent, confidentiality and permissions |
| Timeline | Feasible stages and milestones |
| References | Accurate, consistently formatted sources |

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Choosing a topic that is too broad.
- Treating a literature summary as critical analysis.
- Making claims without credible sources.
- Selecting methods before defining the research question.
- Ignoring ethics, data access or feasibility.
- Promising outcomes that the research design cannot establish.
- Using generated references without checking the original publications.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every university require the same PhD proposal format?

No. Requirements vary by institution, discipline, department and admission stage. Use the current official notification and department guidance.

### How long should a PhD proposal be?

There is no universal length. Follow the word limit and structure set by the target university.

### Do I need a supervisor before submitting?

This depends on the university and programme. Check whether applicants must identify a supervisor, submit a synopsis or complete an interview as part of the process.
